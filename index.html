👋 Hey there!

Hi, I’m Chung-Nan Tsai. Roger works too, if that’s easier. I’m a Product Support Engineer at Lam Research, currently based in Boise, Idaho, though my resume reads more like a flight itinerary: Taiwan, Japan, and now the high desert of the American West. Over a decade-plus, I’ve picked up a somewhat unusual mix of skills spanning Customer Technical Support, AI/LLM applications, Semiconductor Processing, Hardware Systems, Machine Learning, Smart Manufacturing, Data Analytics, and Web Application Development.

On Lam’s North America Product Support team, I’m the last stop when a critical, customer-facing issue won’t quit. Engineering calls me once everything else has been tried. That usually means working closely with design and R&D teams to track down root causes, ship fixes, and keep systems running above 90% availability. Enough data analysis paired with enough hands-on troubleshooting tends to surface patterns; chasing those down has helped cut recurring failures by 35% and push our first-time resolution rate past 90%. Not a bad way to close the gap between “customer is furious” and “problem is actually fixed.”

I like taking messy technical problems and turning them into something practical: solutions that are boring in the best possible way, because they just work. My background covers the full stack of what it takes to keep mission-critical systems alive: software, data, and a fair amount of hands-on troubleshooting. That combination gives me a slightly unusual vantage point; I can follow a problem all the way from a line of code to the customer standing next to a stalled tool.

On the side (though some weeks it stops feeling like the “side”), I’m an active researcher in 🤖 multi-agent LLM systems, with publications touching code vulnerability detection, time series forecasting, and AI-driven diagnostics. It’s a strange but genuinely useful overlap: hands-on engineering by day, poking at frontier AI research whenever the hours allow.

Before Boise, there was Japan. I worked there as a developer and internal Digital Transformation (DX) advocate, which is a fancier way of saying I built production software while trying to convince people that changing how they worked wasn’t as scary as it sounded. I collaborated with teams across Japan, China, and Taiwan to rethink field customer service operations from the ground up, building automation systems that made teamwork smoother and, more importantly, shaved real hours and real costs off the company’s books.

I also spent time chasing new business in Japan’s resurgent semiconductor industry, using Lam’s equipment intelligence AI to land contracts with customers like Kioxia, Sony, JASM, and Rapidus. Each one was a step toward faster smart-manufacturing adoption and sharper competitiveness for them.

Outside the strictly technical work, I’d call myself an innovative team leader. I’ve got a decent nose for business priorities, and I like turning fast-moving goals into outcomes you can actually point to on a balance sheet.

I speak English, Japanese, and Chinese fluently, with a beginner’s grasp of Korean (still a work in progress), and I’ve picked up a genuinely international perspective along the way. I’m authorized to work in both Japan and Taiwan, so wherever the next opportunity happens to be, I’m probably not starting from zero.

お客様向け技術サポートの強化と課題解決への貢献

AIと機械学習を活用して、半導体製造装置が抱えるさまざまな課題にも取り組んでいます。特に力を入れているのは、自律的に稼働する半導体製造装置の開発で、目指しているのは装置の安定稼働と高い歩留まりの実現です。半導体製造装置は非常に精密で複雑なため、トラブル発生時に原因を特定するのは簡単ではなく、対応にもどうしても時間がかかります。そこで、装置自身が異常の原因と場所をすぐに判別し、適切な対応策を提案できる仕組みの開発を進めています。現場の作業効率を上げ、停止時間を最小限に抑えることが、結果的にお客様への技術サポート強化や提供価値の最大化につながると考えています。まだ研究開発の初期段階ではありますが、将来的には完全メンテナンスフリーの自律型装置を実現したいと思い、日々試行錯誤を続けています。

大規模言語モデル(LLM)とLLMエージェントの研究

もう一つ、深く関心を持っているのが大規模言語モデル（LLM）の分野です。特に、複数のLLMが連携して自律的にタスクを遂行する「LLMエージェント」の研究には、かなり本気で取り組んでいます。この技術は、半導体製造装置の自律化において、単なる異常検知や対応策の提案だけでなく、より高度な状況判断や問題解決能力を装置に与えてくれる可能性があると考えています。たとえば、過去の膨大な運転データやメンテナンス記録、技術論文などをLLMエージェントが自律的に読み解き、予兆保全の精度を大きく向上させたり、これまで経験したことのないトラブルにも最適な解決策を導き出す、といった応用が考えられます。将来的には、こうしたLLMエージェントが自律型装置の「脳」となり、複雑な製造プロセス全体を最適化して、本当の意味での「スマートファクトリー」を実現する鍵になればと考えています。

  1. A Sequential Multi-Stage Approach for Code Vulnerability Detection via Confidence and Collaboration-Based Decision Making
    Chung-Nan Tsai, Xin Wang, Cheng-Hsiung Lee, and 1 more author
    In Proceedings of the 2025 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP 2025), Suzhou, China, November 5-9, 2025, 2025
    To Appear. Main Conference, Accepted (Out of 8174 submissions, Acceptance Rate   22%)
    Abs

    While large language models (LLMs) have shown strong capabilities across diverse domains, their application to code vulnerability detection holds great potential for identifying security flaws and improving software safety. In this paper, we propose a sequential multi-stage approach via confidence- and collaboration-based decision making (ConfColl). The system adopts a three-stage sequential classification framework, proceeding through a single agent, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with external examples, and multi-agent reasoning enhanced with RAG. The decision process selects among these strategies to balance performance and cost, with the process terminating at any stage where a high-certainty prediction is achieved. Experiments on a benchmark dataset and a low-resource language demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework in enhancing code vulnerability detection performance.

  2. Leveraging Intra- and Inter-References in Vulnerability Detection Using Multi-Agent Collaboration Based on LLMs
    Chung-Nan Tsai, Jingnan Xie, Chun-Ming Lai, and 1 more author
    Cluster Computing, 2025
    Abs DOI

    As AI technology advances, early detection of code vulnerabilities becomes increasingly critical for preventing exploitation, reducing remediation costs, enhancing user trust, and improving system performance. Recently, Large language Models (LLMs) have shown remarkable performance across various tasks using few-shot learning. This study aims to adopt LLM-based agents in a multi-turn discussion framework enhanced with a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) strategy to improve response quality. Our multi-agent approach transforms a single LLM into a highly collaborative intelligence through multi-turn self-collaboration with diverse personas. By leveraging their combined expertise, this approach enhances the accuracy of code vulnerability detection and improves inference capabilities. To effectively utilize few-shot learning samples, we employ intra-references to extract a small number of similar samples from the training data, and inter-references to obtain samples from external data. The experimental studies are conducted on the TreeVul_Ext dataset, and our model achieves promising results across different evaluation criteria. Furthermore, we apply our model to detect code vulnerabilities in low-resource languages and exhibit competitive performance as well.
